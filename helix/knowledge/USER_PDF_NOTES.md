## Core premise
- Draw on Liquidity (DOL) is the next liquidity pool price is expected to seek.
- Correct DOL depends first on reading **order flow**, then refining with CRT, key levels, prior day/week extremes, range geometry, and IRL/ERL.

## Order flow (HELIX framing)
- **Bullish order flow:** price breaks above highs and rejects below lows.
- **Bearish order flow:** price breaks below lows and rejects above highs.
- Order flow is the prerequisite filter before naming a DOL.

## Candle Range Theory (CRT) — 3-candle model
1. **Generate:** first candle prints a CRT high and CRT low (liquidity banks on both sides).
2. **Purge:** second candle raids one side (often the short-term DOL given current order flow) — e.g. in bearish flow, expect a raid of the CRT high as a short-term DOL.
3. **Neutralize:** if the second candle **closes inside** the first candle’s range, reassign DOL toward the opposite CRT extreme (long-term DOL) and expect expansion that way.
- With bullish flow: short-term DOL ≈ first-candle low; long-term DOL ≈ first-candle high (and the inverse for bearish flow).
- Stack CRT with a **key level** when possible: confluence upgrades the DOL call.

## Previous day / previous week high & low as DOL
Reaction rules summarized from the deck (directional expectation after interaction with PDH/PDL or PWH/PWL):

| Interaction | Typical expectation |
|-------------|---------------------|
| Close above prior extreme | Higher prices; next DOL often the prior high side / continuation toward highs |
| Close below prior extreme | Lower prices; next DOL often the prior low side |
| Large wick above (reject) | Lower prices; DOL toward prior day’s low |
| Large wick below (reject) | Higher prices; DOL toward prior day’s high |
| Close inside prior day’s range (bullish context) | Expect lower / DOL = prior day low |
| Close inside prior day’s range (bearish context) | Expect higher / DOL = prior day high |

- Deck also notes FVGs can appear after decisive closes beyond prior day extremes as continuation imbalance.

## Range dynamics
- Mark **range high, range low, and 50% (equilibrium)**.
- First DOL often the **50%**; second DOL the **range high or low** consistent with order flow.
- Bearish flow: delivery often high → 50% → low; bullish flow: low → 50% → high.
- Combine range + order flow + CRT (+ key level) for higher-confidence targeting.

## IRL vs ERL
- **Internal Range Liquidity (IRL):** FVG / IFVG / order block style inefficiencies inside the dealing range.
- **External Range Liquidity (ERL):** swing highs / swing lows outside / at the edges.
- Moves IRL→ERL or ERL→IRL are highest quality when **CRT and/or SMT** confirm at the IRL touch.
- When price taps IRL with CRT forming *and* SMT present: first DOL ≈ CRT high (context-dependent extreme); second DOL ≈ ERL (e.g. old high/low).

## Premium & discount
- Prefer **sell in premium**, **buy in discount** relative to the dealing range.
- Example path: in premium, short-term DOL may be a discount FVG (IRL); after reaching discount with SMT + CRT inside that FVG/IRL, first DOL = CRT high, second = old high (ERL).

## SMT (as used in the deck)
- Treated as a **divergence / disagreement** confirmation between correlated instruments when price is at IRL — required alongside CRT for stronger IRL→ERL DOL calls.
- Deck does not expand full SMT mechanics; HELIX should treat SMT as cross-pair confirmation, not a standalone entry.

## Operational takeaways for HELIX
- Never name DOL without order-flow context.
- Prefer stacked confluence: order flow + CRT + key level + range 50%/extremes + PDH/PDL rules + premium/discount + SMT.
- Close-vs-wick distinction on prior day/week levels is decision-critical.
- Mid-range without CRT/SMT or clear flow → no forced DOL trade (aligns with HOLD rules in playbook).
